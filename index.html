01About

Technology has always been my favorite way to create.

I've been studying computing since I was 14, when I first started learning how games, digital worlds, and interactive experiences were built. Today I design and develop games, immersive applications, and interactive experiences using Virtual Reality and Augmented Reality.

My work combines development and experience design. I mainly use Unity and C# to build real-time applications, while Figma, web technologies, Xcode, AR Foundation, and OpenXR are part of my workflow depending on the platform and the experience I'm creating.

I've worked professionally with software development since 2014, across different technologies, products, and technical challenges. Over time I found immersive computing to be where I feel most at home: at the intersection of engineering, creativity, interaction, and user experience.

What I enjoy most is turning ideas into things people can play, explore, and experience — projects where technology isn't just infrastructure, but part of the experience itself.

03Craft

How the work gets built.

Flow in Figma, prototype in Unity, test on the device, iterate until the experience feels right. Pick a layer to see what that involves.

Designing the experience first

I use Figma to design interfaces, user flows, interactions, and the structure of an experience before implementation. For immersive applications that means thinking past flat screens: how information, feedback, scale, distance, and space behave inside a three-dimensional environment.

  • Figma
  • User flows
  • UI design
  • Interaction design
  • Prototyping

Unity & C#

Unity is my main development environment. I write C# for application logic, interactions, gameplay systems, reusable components, controllers, data flow, state management, and communication between parts of an application — working closely with Unity's component architecture, prefabs, scenes, and events.

  • C# scripts
  • Components
  • Prefabs
  • Scenes
  • Events
  • Scriptable systems

Flat and spatial interfaces

I build both traditional and immersive interfaces inside Unity. For XR, interface design also involves placement, physical distance, readability, interaction method, and how UI elements exist inside the user's environment.

  • Canvas
  • Screen space
  • World space
  • UI Toolkit

OpenXR & XR Interaction Toolkit

Controller input, hand interaction, object manipulation, ray and grab interactions, locomotion, spatial UI, and interaction feedback — extending into networked rooms and voice presence when the experience is shared. Whenever possible I use OpenXR so projects stay less dependent on a single hardware platform.

  • Meta Quest
  • 6DoF
  • Hand tracking
  • Locomotion
  • Spatial UI
  • Multiplayer

AR Foundation & mobile AR

Experiences that connect real-world environments with interactive digital content: image tracking, plane detection, anchors, camera-based interactions, 3D placement, tracked content, and contextual interfaces.

  • Image tracking
  • Plane detection
  • Anchors
  • Point clouds
  • Android
  • iOS

Interactive systems

A large part of my work happens behind what the user sees: application states, interactions, events, UI behavior, gameplay mechanics, and data flow — structured to stay modular and reusable as prototypes grow into larger applications.

  • State management
  • Event systems
  • Gameplay logic
  • Data flow

3D integration

Scene composition, models, materials, lighting, animation, optimization, and configuring assets so they behave correctly inside Unity. I use Blender when assets need preparation or adjustment before integration.

  • Blender
  • Materials
  • Lighting
  • Animation
  • Optimization

Platforms & deployment

For XR I develop and test directly on devices such as Meta Quest. For browser projects I use Unity WebGL so builds stay playable from the web. For mobile I work with Android and iOS builds, including Xcode for Apple platforms.

  • Meta Quest
  • WebGL
  • Android
  • iOS · iPadOS
  • Xcode
04Process

Idea. Prototype. Experience.

Six steps that repeat, not a straight line — most projects loop back from test to prototype more than once.

01

Understand

Who will use it, where, and what they should feel or accomplish.

02

Design

Flows, interfaces, interaction concepts, references.

03

Prototype

Something interactive early. It answers what documents can't.

04

Build

Systems, interactions, interfaces, technical architecture.

05

Test

On the target device. Scale and comfort don't show in the editor.

06

Iterate

Keep what works, refine the rest, run it again.
